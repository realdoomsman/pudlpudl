Every memecoin needs two kinds of people. The ones who ape in at the top chasing a green candle. And the house that quietly collects on every single trade they make, up or down.

You already know which one you've been.

PUDL flips it. Instead of betting on the coin, you become the liquidity behind it — and you collect the real trading fees the pool generates. Not a promise, not a yield farm emitting a token nobody wants. The actual fees traders pay, every time they buy or sell, landing in your wallet.

The world

The market, from above.

Open PUDL and you're looking down on a world of rivers. Every river is a live memecoin pool. The wider it runs, the more volume is flowing through it. The homes on the banks are other players. This is where you fish.

It's not a metaphor bolted on for looks — it's the actual market, drawn the way it behaves. Money flows downstream. The busiest rivers pay the most. You see the whole thing at a glance instead of squinting at a DEX table.

How you earn

Cast a net.

To earn, you cast a net into a river. That's you providing liquidity to that pool. From that moment, every trade that flows past your net pays you a real fee — on the way up and the way down. Pumping? Fees. Dumping? Fees. Chopping sideways while everyone panics? Fees. You're not predicting the chart. You're taxing the traffic.

Your take shows up live, in SOL, in your own wallet. Every net you cast is a genuine liquidity position on Raydium — pull up your wallet on Solscan and you'll see the position, and the fees it earned. Nothing to take on faith.

Spread the risk

Mix the rivers.

Rivers meet at confluences. So can your nets. Mix two, three, four pools into one bundle and you're no longer tied to a single coin — if one river dries up, the others keep flowing. Or paste any token's contract address and cast straight into its pools, even the freshest launches. You fish where you want.

The engine

Fees flow downhill.

A token launched through PUDL can pledge its creator fees back to the people providing its liquidity — streamed as a boost, instead of pocketed as cash. Why would a creator do that? Because deep liquidity is the only thing that keeps a launch alive. They're buying real, sticky liquidity with fee income that would otherwise just bleed their own chart.

PUDL takes a small cut of everything flowing through, and that cut does two things automatically: it buys back and burns $PUDL, and it floods the flagship $PUDL river — the one that pays liquidity providers the most. The best real yields pull in more liquidity, deeper liquidity pulls in more volume, more volume means more fees, bigger boosts and a bigger burn. The loop tightens on itself.

One thing stays true the whole way down: you earn by providing liquidity, never by passively holding a token. That's the honest version, and it's the only one worth building.

The honest part

Providing liquidity is not free money, and anyone who tells you it is, is selling you something.

If a coin dumps out of your range, your net stops catching fees and you're left holding the weaker side of the pair — the coin that just crashed. That's impermanent loss, the real cost of being the house. Wider nets survive bigger moves and earn less; tighter nets earn more and get shredded faster. You choose. We'd rather you understand the river before you fish it.
